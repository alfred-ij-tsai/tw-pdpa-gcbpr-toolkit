Personal Data Lifecycle Compliance Table

Collection → Processing → Use → Storage → Transmission → Cross-border transfer → Erasure and disposal, an arrangement worked out by the author from the PDPA rather than a statutory classification. For each stage: the statutory obligation, the corresponding Enforcement Rules provision, the supporting records to hold, and the mapping to the Global CBPR System Program Requirements.

The seven stages are an organising scheme, not a statutory classification. The PDPA speaks of collection, processing and use, and defines only those three. The seven stages used here — collection, processing, use, storage, transmission, cross-border transfer, and erasure and disposal — were worked out by the author from the obligations the Act and its Enforcement Rules actually impose, in order to follow the order in which those obligations arise in practice rather than the order in which the articles are numbered. Storage, transmission and disposal are not separate statutory categories: they mostly draw on Article 27, Paragraph 1 and the eleven measures in Article 12, Paragraph 2 of the Enforcement Rules, split out here because they are handled by different people at different times. Nothing turns on the stage a row sits in; the provision cited is what governs. If a different arrangement suits your organisation better, the underlying data is a plain list you can regroup.

How to use this table The table is organised by lifecycle stage. The Global CBPR column is taken from the Annex B Enforcement Map for Chinese Taipei; it is collapsed by default, and expands to show the full question text for each mapped requirement. Incident obligations are in 05 Incident Table; data subject rights are in 04 DSAR Table. For Path A, tick the obligations you want to compare, then export a prompt you can hand to an AI assistant together with the full text of the sector-specific regulation that applies to your organisation. Provisions are quoted verbatim: where a provision addresses government and non-government agencies together (Articles 8, 9 and 12, for example), the parts that concern government agencies only do not apply to non-government agencies. Omissions are marked with an ellipsis (…).

Read before use: this table is a preliminary analysis

What you are looking at is a preliminary analysis. This table reflects only the general rules: the PDPA and its Enforcement Rules. Two further layers of Taiwanese law sit above it and are not reproduced here, and either can change the obligations set out below.
Statute-level special laws. Article 1 of the PDPA provides that other laws prevail where they provide otherwise. Article 177-1 of the Insurance Act, for instance, allows insurance enterprises and their agents and brokers to collect, process and use data on medical records, healthcare and physical examinations with the written consent of the data subject, displacing the ordinary Article 6 analysis for the entire insurance sector. A non-exhaustive catalogue is in Part D.
Sector-specific regulations. Under Article 27, Paragraph 2 of the PDPA, a central government authority may designate non-government agencies to establish a security and maintenance plan; many such regulations are in force and none has an English text you can rely on. They are indexed by authority, non-exhaustively, in Part E.
For a more accurate analysis, identify what applies to your organisation in Parts D and E, obtain the Mandarin text, and use the Path A export panel to generate a prompt that folds it in clause by clause.

Scope This table assumes a non-government agency. The security and maintenance obligations of government agencies fall under Article 18 of the PDPA and are outside its scope. Only provisions that apply to non-government agencies are quoted, and they are quoted verbatim; where a provision also addresses government agencies, the parts that concern government agencies only do not apply.

Special laws and sector regulations This table sets out the PDPA baseline only. Neither the statutes that prevail over the PDPA under the proviso to its Article 1, nor the sector-specific regulations issued under its Article 27, Paragraph 2, are reproduced here. See the non-exhaustive catalogue in 02 Sources and Terminology, Part D, and the non-exhaustive index of sector-specific regulations in Part E, and satisfy yourself independently as to which apply to your sector.

Editorial corrections Where the official English translation contains a typographical error, the corrected word is given and marked [sic], with the original noted in the editorial note on that row.
