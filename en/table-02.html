Sources and Terminology

The instruments relied on in this English edition, a terminology map between the PDPA and the Global CBPR System Program Requirements, and an unofficial translation of the Executive Yuan Directions

How to use this page This English edition quotes only instruments for which an official English translation exists, so that every provision can be traced to a government source. Sector-specific security and maintenance regulations issued under Article 27, Paragraph 2 of the PDPA are therefore not reproduced here; where they matter, 03 Lifecycle Table and the incident draft generator ask you to supply the text of your own sector regulation. The one exception is the Executive Yuan Directions, translated unofficially in Part C because Path C depends on it. The terminology map in Part B is a reading aid: it shows how the same concept is named in each text, and where a concept exists in one and not the other. Part D is different in kind. It catalogues the statute-level special laws that prevail over the PDPA under the proviso to Article 1, and it is expressly not exhaustive: it signposts a layer of law that this toolkit does not attempt to reproduce, so that you can go and find the part of it that applies to you.

AInstruments relied on

Four instruments have official English translations and are quoted directly throughout this edition. A fifth, the Executive Yuan Directions, does not, and is translated unofficially in Part C.

BTerminology map: PDPA and Global CBPR

Read this before using the map. The two texts do not share a vocabulary. The PDPA's official English translation says personal data and data subject; Annex B says personal information and individual, and never uses data subject at all. Three gaps matter more than the naming. First, processing in Article 2, Subparagraph 4 of the PDPA is a narrow, enumerated list of operations performed to establish or use a personal data file; it is not the broad umbrella term familiar from other regimes. Second, the PDPA has no standalone concept of disclosure — disclosing data to a third party is a form of use. Annex B treats disclosure as a distinct act. Third, Annex B contains no equivalent of the Article 6 categories; the word sensitive does not appear in it. Where a cell reads "no counterpart", that is a substantive finding, not an omission.

CExecutive Yuan Directions — unofficial translation

This translation is unofficial. No official English translation of the Directions exists. The Laws & Regulations Database publishes English versions of acts and of regulations issued under statutory authority; the Directions are an internal administrative rule and fall outside that scope. This translation was prepared by the author of this toolkit for reference only and has not been reviewed or approved by any competent authority. The Mandarin text governs. It is included because Path C — the incident response draft — is built on the process these Directions establish, and would be unusable without them.

Translation choices worth flagging. 「要點」is rendered Directions, following the Executive Yuan's usual English style for instruments of this kind. 「個資安維案件」is rendered personal data file security violation case, shortened to security violation case. It is deliberately not rendered as incident or breach, to keep it distinct from the Article 12 concept of an infringement, though the English carries a connotation of established wrongdoing that the Mandarin does not. 「中央目的事業主管機關」follows the PDPA's official translation, central government authority in charge of the industry concerned, shortened after first use to central competent authority. 「重大矚目」is rendered major and high-profile. 「行政檢查」and「行政調查」are kept distinct as administrative inspection (the planned annual exercise under Article 22) and administrative investigation (case-specific).

Promulgation and amendment history. Promulgated by Executive Yuan Letter Yuan-Shou-Fa-Xie-Zi No. 1102001106, 11 August 2021; amended 29 May 2023, 15 August 2023, 28 November 2023, 15 May 2024, and 7 January 2025 (Letter Yuan-Shou-Ge-Zi-Chou-Cha-Zi No. 1140500004).

DSpecial laws that prevail over the PDPA

This list is not exhaustive. It records the statute-level special laws the author has identified as bearing on non-government agencies, and there will be others that are not listed here. Regulations made under an enabling provision of a statute are a separate layer and are not listed here at all. Treat it as a prompt to investigate your own sector, not as a complete answer. If you are subject to a statute that displaces or supplements the PDPA and it does not appear below, the omission means only that it has not yet been catalogued.

Why this matters. Article 1 of the PDPA provides that the collection, processing and use of personal data shall be governed by the Act, except where otherwise provided by other laws. That proviso is the gateway: wherever another statute lays down its own rule on personal data, that rule prevails. For non-government agencies these statutes cluster in finance, healthcare, employment, telecommunications, and the protection of vulnerable people and victims of crime. A first-pass estimate puts the number at somewhere between twenty-five and thirty-five.

How to read the entries. The effect described for each entry is the author's one-line summary, not a quotation, and no statutory text is reproduced in this Part. That is deliberate. Reproducing two or three of these statutes in full while omitting the rest would repeat the very problem this Part exists to solve, and several have no official English translation to reproduce. Read the statute itself before relying on any entry. Where the article number is given, it is the entry point rather than the whole of the relevant text.

Finding the official text. No links are given in this Part, deliberately. Copy the English or Mandarin name of a statute and search for it yourself in the Laws & Regulations Database, so that what you read is the version in force on the day you read it rather than the version current when this page was written. Where the English and Mandarin versions differ, the Mandarin version governs.

Three categories, three paths. The grouping is by what the special law does rather than by industry, because each category bears on a different part of this toolkit. D1 changes the lawful basis or the consent requirement, and so bears on Path A. D2 changes how a data subject right is performed or how long you have, and so bears on Path B. D3 adds a duty of confidentiality or a prohibition on disclosure, and so bears on Paths A and C. A statute may appear in more than one category where different articles do different things: the Human Subjects Research Act, for instance, appears in D1 for its consent provisions and in D3 for its non-disclosure provisions.

What this catalogue leaves out. This toolkit deals with administrative compliance obligations and the administrative supervision that follows a failure to meet them. Statutes governing criminal procedure are therefore outside its scope, even where they bear heavily on personal data. The Communication Security and Surveillance Act is the clearest example: it regulates the interception of communications and the obtaining of communications records in the course of criminal investigation, and is not catalogued here.

ESector-specific regulations, by authority

This Part is an index, not a source. It exists so that you can work out which regulation applies to your organisation. It gives names only. No provision of any regulation listed here is reproduced anywhere in this toolkit, and none of these regulations has an English text you can rely on.
Treat this index as non-exhaustive. These regulations are numerous, are issued and amended by some twenty separate authorities, and change often enough that any snapshot will drift. Confirm against the Laws & Regulations Database rather than relying on the index alone.

Why you need this. Under Article 27, Paragraph 2 of the PDPA, a central government authority in charge of an industry may designate non-government agencies to establish a security and maintenance plan for personal data files. Many such regulations are in force. Where one applies to you, it will set the recipient, deadline and content of any incident report you must make, and may impose requirements stricter than the PDPA baseline used throughout this toolkit.

How to use it. Find your authority, then find the regulation covering your industry. Copy the Mandarin name and search it in the Laws & Regulations Database. You do not need to read Mandarin to do this: the Mandarin name is the search key, and the English name beside it is there only so that you can tell which entry is yours. Once you have the Mandarin text, paste it into the prompt generated by Path A or Path C, which will compare it against the baseline clause by clause.

The English names are for identification only. Most are the English names published by the government and are reproduced verbatim; the remainder were rendered by the author. Neither may be cited as a source, and the Mandarin text governs in every case. The published English names are not consistent with one another: the same term 個人資料檔案 appears as both Personal Information Files and Personal Data Files, and 安全維護計畫 appears as Security Maintenance Plan, Security Protection Plan, Plan of Security Measures and Security Assurance Plan. They are reproduced as published rather than harmonised, because that is the wording you will encounter when you search.

Versions relied on Personal Data Protection Act: official English translation, amended 31 May 2023 (provisions in force). The amendments of 11 November 2025 are not yet in force; their enforcement date is to be set by the Executive Yuan. Enforcement Rules: official English translation, amended 2 March 2016.

Statute-level special laws Part D is a non-exhaustive catalogue compiled by the author. The effects described there are the author's summaries and not quotations, and no statutory text from those laws is reproduced anywhere in this toolkit. Identify the statutes applicable to your own sector independently, and read them in the original.

Terminology inconsistency in the source texts Both the specific purposes and classification instrument and the Global CBPR Annex B Enforcement Map refer to the statute as the "Personal Information Protection Act", an earlier rendering. The current official translation of the Act uses "Personal Data Protection Act". Titles are reproduced here as they appear in the source.

Sector-specific regulations Regulations issued by central competent authorities under Article 27, Paragraph 2 of the PDPA are outside the scope of this English edition, as they have no official English translations. The Mandarin edition of this toolkit contains a full inventory of some sixty-five such regulations across thirty authorities.
