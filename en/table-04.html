Data Subject Access Request (DSAR) Table

The five rights under Article 3 — inquiry and review, a copy, supplement or correction, cessation of collection, processing or use, and erasure — with the response obligations, time limits and grounds for refusal for each, mapped to the Global CBPR System Program Requirements

How to use this table Part I (procedural foundations) and Part VIII (records and audit) apply to all five rights; Parts II to VII set out the obligations specific to each. The decision period depends on which article the request is made under: requests under Article 10 — inquiry, review and copies — carry fifteen days, extendable once by up to fifteen; requests under Article 11 — supplement, correction, cessation and erasure — carry thirty days, extendable once by up to thirty (Article 13). Provisions are quoted only to the extent that they apply to non-government agencies. To generate a printable checklist and calculated deadlines for a particular case, use the DSAR draft generator. General lifecycle obligations are in 03 Lifecycle Table; incident obligations are in 05 Incident Table.

Check first whether a special law displaces the PDPA. Article 1 of the PDPA provides that the collection, processing and use of personal data shall be governed by the Act, except where otherwise provided by other laws. Several statutes change how a data subject right is performed, or how long you have to perform it. The Medical Care Act is the clearest example: Article 71 requires a copy of a patient's medical records to be provided without undue delay, setting no fixed period and providing no ground of refusal, and Article 68 requires an amended entry to be struck through with a line rather than erased. This table reproduces no statute other than the PDPA and its Enforcement Rules, so identify any special law applicable to your sector and read it alongside. A non-exhaustive catalogue is in Part D. A sector-specific regulation made under Article 27, Paragraph 2 of the PDPA may add further requirements; they are indexed by authority in Part E, non-exhaustively.

Decision periods Article 13, Paragraph 1: a request under Article 10 must be granted or refused within fifteen days, extendable once by no more than fifteen days, with written notice of the reason to the requester. Article 13, Paragraph 2: a request under Article 11 must be granted or refused within thirty days, extendable once by no more than thirty days, on the same terms. Periods run from the day after receipt; where the final day falls on a holiday it is postponed under Article 48, Paragraph 4 of the Administrative Procedure Act.

Grounds for refusal The three grounds in the proviso to Article 10 apply only to requests to inquire, review or obtain a copy — Article 3, Subparagraphs 1 and 2. Refusal of a request to supplement, correct, cease or erase must be assessed under the main text and provisos of Article 11, not under Article 10. Every refusal should state its reasons in writing and be retained on file.

Scope This table assumes a non-government agency. The obligations of government agencies fall under Article 18 of the PDPA and are outside its scope.
