Personal Data Incident Table

The six-stage handling process established by the Executive Yuan Directions, with the corresponding obligations under the PDPA and its Enforcement Rules, mapped to the Global CBPR System Program Requirements

How to use this table The structure follows the process for handling security violation cases under the Directions for the Executive Yuan and Its Subordinate Agencies on Liaison Operations for the Implementation of Personal Data Protection (amended 7 January 2025). It was built outward from the fields of the Supervision and Notification Record Form in Annex 1 to those Directions, rather than from the statute towards the form. Provisions are quoted only to the extent that they apply to non-government agencies: where a provision addresses government and non-government agencies together, such as Article 12, only the part applicable to non-government agencies is reproduced. To generate a printable checklist, timeline and AI prompt for a particular case, use the incident draft generator. General lifecycle obligations are in 03 Lifecycle Table.

Two things to hold apart before reading this table. First, the Executive Yuan Directions have no official English translation. Every passage from them shown below is an unofficial translation prepared for this toolkit; the Mandarin text governs. The full bilingual text is in 02 Sources and Terminology. Second, and more consequentially, the 24-hour and 72-hour deadlines in the Directions are obligations of the central government authority, running from the moment that authority becomes aware of the case. They are not your organisation's reporting deadlines. Under the provisions of the PDPA currently in force, a non-government agency has no obligation to report an incident to any authority at all; only the obligation under Article 12 to notify affected data subjects. Where a reporting obligation exists, it comes from the sector-specific regulation that applies to your organisation.

Special laws may change the analysis. Article 1 of the PDPA provides that the collection, processing and use of personal data shall be governed by the Act, except where otherwise provided by other laws. Several statutes impose duties of confidentiality or prohibit the disclosure of identifying information, and these bear directly on how an incident is contained and how data subjects are notified: Article 48, Paragraph 2 of the Banking Act on customer data; Article 23 of the Physicians Act; Article 14 of the HIV Infection Control and Patient Rights Protection Act; Article 69 of the Protection of Children and Youths Welfare and Rights Act; and the victim-identity provisions of the Sexual Assault Crime Prevention Act and the Domestic Violence Prevention Act. None is reproduced in this toolkit. A non-exhaustive catalogue is in Part D; sector-specific regulations made under Article 27, Paragraph 2 are indexed by authority, non-exhaustively, in Part E.

Whose deadlines these are The 24 hours (major and high-profile cases) and 72 hours (ordinary cases) set by the Directions are the deadlines by which the central government authority in charge of the industry concerned must notify the PDPC Preparatory Office. They run from the moment that authority receives a notification or a copy of one, or otherwise becomes aware of the case. A non-government agency's own reporting deadline, where one exists, is set by the sector-specific regulation applicable to it. The two must not be conflated.

What makes a case major and high-profile Point 2, Paragraph 2 of the Directions: a case that has attracted the attention of the Executive Yuan, the Legislative Yuan or the Control Yuan; or one that has received prominent media coverage, such as coverage in the national pages of print media or feature discussion in electronic media.

Parallel reporting tracks Where a personal data incident is also a cybersecurity incident, the procedures under the PDPA and under the Cyber Security Management Act run separately. The recipients, deadlines and formats differ, and a failure in either track is an independent violation.

Special laws Statutes that prevail over the PDPA under the proviso to its Article 1 are not reproduced here; see the non-exhaustive catalogue in 02 Sources and Terminology, Part D, and the index of sector-specific regulations in Part E. The confidentiality and non-disclosure provisions in Part D are particularly relevant to incident handling, since they may restrict what may be said, to whom, and in what form.

Scope This table assumes a non-government agency. Incidents at government agencies are handled under Article 18 of the PDPA and the provisions of the Directions applicable to government agencies, and are outside its scope.
